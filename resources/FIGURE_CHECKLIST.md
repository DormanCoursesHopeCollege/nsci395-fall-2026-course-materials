# Figure checklist

## Scientific meaning

- What is one point, line, bar, or row?
- What comparison is the figure meant to show?
- Are raw observations visible when they matter?
- Is uncertainty shown and defined?
- Does the figure hide variation through averaging?

## Labels

- Label both axes.
- Include units.
- Identify conditions without relying only on color.
- Use a title only when it adds information.
- Make text readable at normal notebook size.

## Integrity

- Do not truncate an axis in a way that distorts the comparison.
- Do not connect observations with a line unless their order has meaning.
- Do not use a color scale whose meaning is unclear.
- Do not claim statistical or causal evidence that the figure does not provide.

## Caption

State:

1. what was measured or simulated;
2. what each condition or panel represents;
3. the main visible result;
4. any summary or uncertainty calculation needed to interpret it.
